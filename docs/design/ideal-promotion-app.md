# The ideal product-promotion application: research and what to build here

Status: **research and proposal, nothing decided or built.** Written 2026-11-27 at the owner's request ("how would you create the ideal application for promoting products: choose the functions, processes, scenarios and strategies, and write out what can be done in the application"). Based on three reads done that day: the application at 0.50.0, the leading tools of each category, and the promotion practices of 2025-2026. Evidence marks: **V** = read on the source's own page, **S** = secondary source, **P** = practitioner knowledge without a study. Sources are in section 10. The publishing side (schedule, queue, official APIs) has its own plan, `publishing-api-and-schedule-plan.md`, and is only referred to here.

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

Every tool on the market does one stretch of the road: schedulers stop at "published", listening tools stop at the alert, analytics stop at the chart, and the "AI marketer" products are supervised drafting that needs 15-45 minutes of review a day and forgets what worked (V, reviews). The ideal application for one owner with several small products is **one closed loop on the owner's own accounts**:

**Understand → Choose → Make → Publish → Talk → Measure → Learn → (back to Choose)**

The AI proposes and prepares at every step; the owner reads, decides and presses; the application counts and remembers. Promotion Desk already has the first five steps in some form. The last two, Measure and Learn, do not exist at all, and they are what turns activity into promotion.

## 2. What the research says (the findings that shape the design)

| # | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Positioning comes before posting: alternatives, what is unique, the value of it, who cares most, the category | V (Dunford's method; no outcome data) |
| 2 | Test about three channels cheaply, then put the effort into one or two; spreading over many is the commonest failure of a solo owner | S (Bullseye) |
| 3 | A week with no posts falls below the account's own growth baseline (161,000 profiles); very high frequency lowers reach per post | V (Buffer) |
| 4 | "Best time to post" and hashtags are close to noise; consistency matters more. Instagram caps hashtags at 5 and they do not raise reach | V, S |
| 5 | Accounts whose author replies to comments show more engagement against their own baseline: Threads +42%, LinkedIn +30%, Instagram +21%, Facebook +9.5%, X +8% (association, not proven cause) | V (Buffer) |
| 6 | Search-like surfaces keep working for months: Pinterest (links encouraged, keywords in title, description and board; seasonal content 4-6 weeks ahead), Etsy, the own site, YouTube | V (Pinterest), S |
| 7 | External links cost reach on Facebook (97.3% of post views carry no outside link; some Pages capped at two link posts a month), are the weakest format on X and Threads, are not clickable in Instagram captions, and are welcome on Pinterest | V, S |
| 8 | Identical cross-posts and reposts are penalised: Instagram removes re-uploaders from recommendations (30 Apr 2026); LinkedIn demotes recycled posts, pods and automated comments (Mar 2026) | V |
| 9 | Automated behaviour is what gets accounts lost: keyword auto-replies, bulk DMs, auto-like and auto-follow, replies from accounts that are not yours. Reddit removes about 100,000 accounts a day and treats posting speed as a signal | V, S |
| 10 | Obviously AI-written content lowers trust (Gartner 2026: 57% trust brand messaging less) and LinkedIn down-ranks suspected AI comments | S |
| 11 | AI search (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) cites Reddit and YouTube most, prefers earned media and big brands; Google says it is "still SEO": original first-hand content with numbers and quotes | V |
| 12 | The lightest measurement that works: a tagged link per post, one privacy analytics tool joined to payments, and one question "where did you hear about us?" | V (tools), S |
| 13 | With tens of posts one can see a repeated direction over five or more comparable posts and order-of-magnitude outliers; one cannot conclude a best hour or a wording effect | reasoning, no source |
| 14 | Nobody tells the owner that a post was silently removed, nobody keeps the people met in communities, nobody serves several small products of one person, and nobody attributes an app-store or craft-shop sale | V (tool pages) |

Promotion Desk already answers part of 14: it re-reads a sent post for three days and shows "Removed" with the community's own words (Reddit).

## 3. Functions by step of the loop

"Have" is what works at 0.50.0. "Add" is the proposal; the number is its place in section 7.

| Step | Have | Add |
|---|---|---|
| **Understand** | Sources (site, text, documents, pictures, code); passport of eight sections with facts, assumptions and contradictions, approved by the owner; goals as text; voice per login; languages per channel | Positioning as five approved answers (4). A bank of the audience's own words, questions and objections, collected from the Feed with the link to each (4). Goals with a number and a date (1) |
| **Choose** | Channel ideas from the AI's knowledge; a catalog of 11 platforms; an AI plan per channel; a launch path | Channel trials: hypothesis, length, hours, threshold, verdict (3). A limit on channels in work, the rest parked (3). Channel ideas found by looking, not only from the model's memory (8) |
| **Make** | AI draft with search; post from the owner's files; change a part; versions per channel; preview per platform; picture and video library; AI pictures; content mix (drafts only) | Themes and campaigns: one main piece, native versions per channel, a calendar (5). Seasonal calendar per product (5). Translate my post; topic tags and keywords where the platform uses them; carousels (10) |
| **Publish** | Approval bound to the exact content; send gate; Reddit by connector, others by AI browser on a press; schedule on read Reddit only; removal re-check | The publishing plan: a time and a queue for every channel, work in the background, official APIs (6). A minimum weekly plan and a "week with no time" mode (6) |
| **Talk** | Reddit: comments, replies to me, mentions, AI triage, drafted replies; Feed with threads, read, translate, explain, analysis; comment from the Feed | Comments under the owner's own posts on every channel in one Inbox (6, 7). A reply window after each post (7). Testimonials kept with permission (7). The people met: handle, thread, what was said, what is owed (7) |
| **Measure** | Reddit community and account totals; a funnel found → sent; sends per day | A tagged link per post (1). Likes, views and comments per post (1, already planned). Visits, sign-ups and sales per post from the site's analytics (1). Each account against its own baseline (2) |
| **Learn** | Rules about how to work a site, kept from the owner's answers | A weekly review: what ran, what moved, one proposed change (2). Results feed the next week's topics, formats and channels (2). Trial verdicts (3) |

## 4. Processes (what the owner does, and when)

| Rhythm | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | 10-15 min | Inbox: approve or edit what the AI prepared (posts for the queue, replies to new comments, comments to threads worth joining). Nothing else is required |
| **After each post** | 48 hours | The reply window: every comment under the post arrives with a drafted answer; a post that took off raises its own item (scenario 3) |
| **Weekly review** | 20 min | One page per product: what went out, what it brought (visits, sign-ups, sales, comments), how each channel stands against its own baseline, removed posts, repeated questions; one change proposed by the AI with its reason; the owner accepts or not. The next week's queue is filled from it |
| **Monthly** | 30 min | Trials reach their verdict (keep, stop, extend). The seasonal calendar for the next six weeks is approved. The AI-answer check: a fixed list of 10-20 questions a buyer would ask, who is named and which sources are cited |
| **Per theme** | as needed | Campaign: theme → main piece → native versions per channel → reply window → recap in the weekly review |
| **Per new channel** | 3 weeks | Trial written before it starts: what we expect, how many hours, what number means "keep" |
| **Per new account** | 1-2 weeks | Warm-up: comments only, at a person's pace, before the first post |

## 5. Scenarios

Each is a ready flow in the application: a trigger, what the AI prepares, what the owner decides, how the result is judged.

| # | Trigger | The AI prepares | The owner decides | Judged by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A new product with no audience | Passport from the sources, positioning answers, three channel trials | Approves positioning and trials | Three trials running |
| 2 | A channel trial ends | The numbers against the threshold written at the start | Keep, stop or extend | Threshold met or not |
| 3 | A post took off | Replies, a follow-up, versions for other channels; checks the link opens | Which of them go out | Visits and sales from that post's link |
| 4 | A community removed a post | What the community wrote under it; a changed version or a note to leave | Repost, write to moderators, leave | No second removal there |
| 5 | Someone asks what the product answers | A helpful answer in the thread's language | Send, edit or skip | Votes on the reply, clicks |
| 6 | A seasonal date in five weeks | Pins and posts now, a build-up schedule | Approves the set | Saves and visits in the season |
| 7 | A week with no time | The minimum: one re-cut of an earlier piece per channel in work, plus reply drafts | One approval | No empty week |
| 8 | A negative comment | Its kind (objection, defect, provocation), a calm reply, a record in the objection bank | Reply, ignore, fix the product | The thread did not grow |
| 9 | A software launch | Checklist per venue with the venue's own rules (Show HN: something to try, no requests for votes), posts, the reply window | Date and wording | Sign-ups in the launch week |
| 10 | A new account | A warm-up plan of comments | The pace | The first post stays up |
| 11 | Reach fell | Comparison with the account's own baseline: formats, share of link posts, state of the account | Change the format or wait | Back to baseline |
| 12 | A happy customer's message | A request for permission; the testimonial kept with its source | Where to use it | Testimonials collected |
| 13 | The same question a third time | An FAQ entry and a post that answers it | Approve | Fewer repeats |
| 14 | The monthly AI-answer check | Who is named for each question, which sources are cited | Which gap to fill | Mentions over months |
| 15 | The main piece is finished | Native versions per channel and their places in the queue | Edits and approves | Versions out, visits to the piece |
| 16 | A shop listing has views and no sales | Comparison of first photo, title, tags and price with neighbours in search | Which change | Sales of that listing |
| 17 | A channel went quiet for a month | The trend; a proposal to park it and re-run the choice of channels | Park or keep | Hours moved to what works |
| 18 | A person met before appears again | What was said last time and what was promised | How to answer | — |

## 6. Strategies

Ranked for one owner with no budget. The first six are worth encoding as processes; the last two are worth *not* building.

| # | Strategy | In the application | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Positioning first | The positioning step of the passport | V method |
| 2 | One or two channels after short trials | Trials and the limit on channels in work | S |
| 3 | Search-like evergreen surfaces: Pinterest, the shop's own search, the site, YouTube | Keywords and boards for pins; the seasonal calendar; the site piece as the campaign's centre | V |
| 4 | Helpful participation in existing communities | Feed, triage and comments (exist); the ratio of own-link to other activity per account, shown as a number | S; high removal risk |
| 5 | Answer every comment in the first 48 hours | The reply window | V, association |
| 6 | A steady, sustainable rhythm | Queue, minimum weekly plan, low-time mode | V |
| 7 | A native original per channel (carousels on LinkedIn and Instagram, text on X, vertical image or video on Pinterest) | Versions per channel (exist); format advice from the account's own results | V |
| 8 | Output of the product that people send to each other | Advice to the owner, outside the application (a chart or card made to be shared) | S, case only |
| 9 | A seasonal calendar 4-6 weeks ahead | The calendar per product | V |
| 10 | First-hand content with numbers, for search and AI answers | The main piece of a campaign; the AI-answer check | V |
| 11 | Testimonials collected honestly | The testimonial store | V rules, effect unknown |
| 12 | An email list as a channel the owner owns | Not proposed now (the application sends no mail); a question for the owner | not researched |
| 13 | One-off launches | The venue checklist, for software products only | S |
| 14 | Optimising the hour of posting; hashtags | **Not built**: a sensible default window is enough | V |

By kind of product, as understood from the channels in use:

- **A consumer niche product (Natal Mystic).** The calendar writes the content plan by itself: moon phases, sign seasons, retrogrades, each prepared weeks ahead for Pinterest and days ahead elsewhere (P; lead time V). Questions from the communities are the richest source of topics (the Feed analysis already finds unanswered questions). The strongest known growth in this niche came from results people share with friends (S), which is a property of the product, not of the posts.
- **A handmade or art product (Sirius Art).** Process video with the result shown in the first seconds; Pinterest as long-lived search; a gift calendar worked back 4-6 weeks; the shop's own search (title, tags, attributes, recent reviews and answers) if a marketplace is used (S, P). A sale there is invisible to site analytics, so the question "where did you hear about us?" matters most here.
- **A small software tool.** The launch sequence with each venue's rules, founder's notes as material, a few honest comparison pages (Google treats templated hundreds as abuse, V), communities.

| **4** | **Positioning and the audience's words.** Five positioning answers proposed from the passport and approved; a bank of questions, objections and phrases taken from the Feed with links; three repeats propose an FAQ entry and a post | Drafts that say why this product and not the alternative, in the audience's words | — | Medium |
| **5** | **Themes, campaigns and the seasonal calendar.** A theme with its main piece and native versions; a calendar per product (astronomical events computed, gift dates listed, the owner's own dates), each with its lead time per channel | The plan for six weeks ahead prepared by the AI; nothing seasonal is late | 6 for the queue | Medium |

## 8. What the ideal application does not do

Sold by other tools and the reason accounts are lost (V, platform rules; S, enforcement reports):

- automatic replies by keyword, and any comment sent without the owner's approval of its text;
- bulk or automatic direct messages;
- automatic likes, votes, follows, connection requests (already blocked in the code);
- posting from accounts that are not the owner's, or several accounts speaking in one discussion (the one-voice rule exists);
- engagement pods, requests for votes, invented testimonials or reviews;
- identical text sent to every channel;
- generated text on venues that forbid it (Hacker News says so in its guidelines).

Already decided by the owner and not proposed again: no reading of community rules, no warning and no block from them (DEC-050); no rule that the author must state a connection to the product (DEC-048); no automatic standalone posts; no unified posting service that holds the accounts' tokens; no Brand entity; no evasion of a site's checks.

One finding sits against DEC-048 and is recorded here as a fact only: the FTC's guidance says a recommendation of one's own product must make the connection clear in the message itself when it is not obvious (V). The decision stays the owner's; the application proposes nothing about it.

## 9. Questions for the owner

1. Which analytics do the products' sites use today, if any, and where do sales happen (own site with a payment service, an app store, a marketplace)? Item 1 depends on it.
2. Is the order in section 7 right, or should conversations (7) or the calendar (5) come before measurement?
3. How many channels per product should be in work at once (the research suggests one or two after trials)?
4. Is an email list wanted as a channel? It would be the first channel the owner owns, and the application sends no mail today.
5. Is software among the products to promote (items 12 and scenario 9)?
6. The "week with no time" mode sends a re-cut of an earlier piece: acceptable, or should an empty week stay empty?
