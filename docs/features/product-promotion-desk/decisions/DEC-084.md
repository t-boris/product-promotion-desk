## Context

On LinkedIn the application worked only in an account's browser. The plan's phase J asks for publishing through LinkedIn's API for a personal profile. LinkedIn's pages (saved 2026-11-28) say what an app anyone can make may do: with the products "Share on LinkedIn" and "Sign In with LinkedIn using OpenID Connect" it posts as the member (POST /v2/ugcPosts, pictures and a video uploaded from a local file through /v2/assets) and reads the member's name and id (/v2/userinfo). Reading the member's posts, comments and numbers is closed to such an app, a company page needs a reviewed product, and a token (60 days, made in the portal's token generator) is not renewed for such an app. Nothing was tried with a real app.

## Alternatives

1. One account that holds both a browser sign-in and a key, so the same profile is posted to through the API and its comments are watched and answered in the browser (the plan's words 'comments stay with the AI browser'): the right end state, but it changes the account's page and how a send is routed, so it waits for the owner's word
2. The application's own sign-in window with LinkedIn (OAuth with the app's id and secret): no gain for one owner, the token still lasts 60 days and is not renewed
3. The newer /rest/posts API: needs a product that LinkedIn reviews

## Decision

A LinkedIn account can be added as "Through a developer app". Its one key is the LinkedIn access token of the token generator, kept in the vault. Connector linkedin-api: a channel is the address of a personal profile (linkedin.com/in/name); a post is words up to 3000 characters, 1-9 pictures (JPEG, PNG, GIF) or one MP4 video up to 200 MB, public; words that end with an address are sent as an article share so LinkedIn shows the page's card; a video is waited for until LinkedIn says it is ready. Replies are refused in words. The channel is one the application does not read: it is for the owner's own posts, has no numbers, and is never watched in a browser, because an account that works through an API has none (a channel's "watched in the browser" is now true only for an account that works in a browser). A post whose answer was lost stays "result unknown" until the owner says whether it is there. A key that ends and is not renewed is a general thing (ApiAccess.keyDays): the application counts the days from the day the key was saved, shows the day on the account's page, and puts an item into the Inbox a week ahead and after the end. The vault now takes keys up to 4096 characters.

## Reason

It makes LinkedIn posts go out by themselves at their time, which is what the plan's phase J asks, with what LinkedIn gives to anyone without a review. Saying plainly that nothing is read is truer than a channel that looks watched and never shows a comment.

## Consequences

Unproven until the owner has a LinkedIn app: every request, the names of the portal's screens, whether a self-serve token may ask the state of an uploaded video (if not, a video fails with LinkedIn's words and nothing is published), whether several pictures go in one such post, whether an article share shows the card, the shape of a refusal. LinkedIn does not tell the application the address of the token's profile, so the first post shows whether the channel's address is the right one. Not built: company pages through the API, comments and numbers, a video over 200 MB, visibility for connections only.
