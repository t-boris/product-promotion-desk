## Context

Until 0.26.3 the AI had one hidden page per account for every connector read and every mission. The owner has three Reddit channels on one account and said (2026-11-21): "you open one, it checks as if it were another... one window for every call you need, then you close it. That's all", and "do not move the focus to the browser you open". On Electron, the AI's step driver called contents.focus() before every key, type, click and scroll step; on macOS that makes the AI's window the active one and pulls the owner out of what they are doing (checked on Electron 45: showInactive keeps the owner's window focused, contents.focus() takes it).

## Alternatives

1. Keep one AI page per account and close it after every task: fixes the leftover window and the focus, not the interleaving of connector reads with a mission's page.
2. Several missions at once per account (one per channel): needs run state, Stop and questions per run instead of per account; left for later, a second look on the same account still waits for the first.

## Decision

Every call of the AI (a connector read, a login check, an assessment) works in a page and a window made for that call and closed when it ends. A mission (watch, look around, post, join, sign-in) keeps one page and window for all its steps (an AgentTask), shown without the focus when it starts and closed when it ends, however it ends. The owner's own windows (Open browser) are unchanged: one per address, the same address raises the window already open. The AI's step driver never calls contents.focus(); a window comes forward only when the owner asks (Take control, or the AI's question). Everything else stays per account: one sign-in (partition or Chrome profile), one queue of turns, one mission at a time, Take control stops every page, and the identity epoch.

## Reason

A page of its own per call removes the confusion between channels of one account (a read for one channel can no longer move the page another is using), and windows that close themselves leave nothing behind. Dropping contents.focus() removes the focus theft; typing, clicking and scrolling were checked to work in a window shown without the focus, and a real-Electron run of the coexistence probe and a focus probe pass.

## Consequences

A call on a Chrome account opens and closes a Chrome window (behind the owner's) each time; Electron accounts do this hidden. A page report taken while the AI is idle finds no AI page. Whether starting Chrome itself takes the focus on macOS is not checked. Two AI looks on one account still go one after another.
