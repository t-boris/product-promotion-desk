## Context

On Instagram the application worked only in an account's browser: a post went out by the AI browser on the owner's press, and the owner confirmed each post because the application could not read it back. Meta's Instagram API with Instagram Login lets a professional account publish, read comments and read numbers with a token the app dashboard generates. Its API takes every picture and video only from an address on the internet, which the place for files (DEC-081) now gives. Built from Meta's saved pages of 2026-11-27 (host graph.instagram.com, v27.0); nothing was tried with a real app.

## Alternatives

1. Unified Login or Facebook Login (graph.facebook.com): needs a business portfolio or a Facebook Page, and a different token; it would allow a video from a local file through the resumable upload
2. Asking for the app secret and exchanging a short token, as for Threads: the dashboard already gives a long one, and Instagram cannot be asked which kind a token is
3. Converting a PNG to JPEG inside the application: the core has no picture converter, and a new library needs the owner's word
4. Reading the token's end through a debug call: Meta's Instagram pages document none

## Decision

An Instagram account can be added as "Through a developer app (Instagram's own API)". Its one key is the access token of the app dashboard ("Generate token"), kept in the vault. Connector instagram-api: a post is one JPEG picture, one video (sent as a reel with share_to_feed) or 2-20 of them as a carousel, with the caption; each file is put on the place for files for the send and deleted after it; the link is never put into a caption. The comments under the account's posts are read from Instagram each time (the 50 newest of a post, with their replies); a reply goes under a comment and a comment under the own post. Likes and comments are read from the post, views from its insights, followers from the profile. Because Meta's pages name no call that says when such a token ends, the application remembers when a key was saved or renewed (table api_key_times) and gives those times to the connector: an Instagram token is renewed a day after it was saved and a week after each renewal. The addresses use the account's user_id, as the publishing guide does; reads carry the token as a parameter and writes in the Authorization header with a JSON body, as the pages show each. A picture that is not JPEG, a post of words only and a missing place for files are said before the owner approves and refused before anything is asked of Instagram. A post Instagram says it published without its caption is reported as "result unknown" with those words, never as published.

## Reason

It makes Instagram posts go out by themselves at their time and makes comments and numbers readable without a browser, the same way as a Telegram bot, a Facebook Page and a Threads profile. Instagram Login needs no Facebook Page and its dashboard gives a 60-day token directly, so one owner needs no sign-in flow and no app secret here. Renewing by remembered times is the only way that needs nothing undocumented.

## Consequences

Unproven until the owner has a Meta app with the Instagram use case and a bucket: every request, the names of the dashboard's screens, whether Instagram reads a file from an address with a signature in its query, which of the two ids the addresses take, whether a picture's container is ready at once, whether a renewed token is another word, the shape of a refusal, whether posts made before "Go live" are public. Not built: stories, alt text, tags of people and places, hiding or deleting a comment, comments older than the 50 newest, the publishing quota read. A PNG picture must be saved as JPEG by the owner. The same key-times table can later show a key's end date on the account's page.
