## Context

0.36.0 (DEC-049) added "Send anyway" for a comment held because a community's rules appeared to forbid automation or AI-generated content: the application read the rules of a Reddit community, guessed from their wording, stored a restriction on publish_post and publish_reply and a note about promotion, and held the owner's own press. On 2026-11-21 (log 15:21Z, "Send not allowed (channel_restricted)") the owner pressed Send now on a Feed comment and was told "Not posted. The community's rules do not allow this: Community rules appear to forbid automation or AI-generated content — "No AI generated content."". The owner's answer, relayed through the coordinator: no block, no warning, no hint ("без подсказки"), and "remove the note about promotion in the rules as well" ("Убери ещё и пометку про рекламу в правилах"). The owner decides what to post; the AI is eyes and helper (lesson 300: a rule written to protect the owner grew into a block on the owner's own text). A guess from rules text is a heuristic, and the one thing it produced was a refusal of the owner's own decision.

## Alternatives

1. Keep the detection as a warning without a block (rejected by the owner: no hint at all)
2. Keep Send anyway for the remaining case (rejected: nothing a community's rules say reaches the window any more, so there is nothing to let through; a restriction the site itself enforces cannot be let through by a button)
3. Keep the rules reader and show the rules on the Channels screen (rejected: no consumer is left; the owner reads a community's rules on the community's page, one press away in the app's browser)
4. Edit migration 318 or drop approvals.acknowledged_json (rejected: an applied migration is never edited; the column stays, unused, with its default)

## Decision

1) Community rules restrict nothing and warn about nothing, neither about AI/automation nor about promotion. The application no longer reads a community's rules text at all: the Reddit connector does not call the rules page, the universal connector has no rulesUrl/rulesSelector, the rules reader (src/connectors/rules-text.ts) and the 'rules' capability are deleted. 2) What the site itself enforces still restricts: a banned account, an archived community (unavailable), a restricted community the account is not approved in (post only), not joined (Membership row), plus the gate's other holds (account not ready, channel paused or not active, publish capability unavailable or needs the owner, AI browser held by the owner). These are refused with their own plain sentence and the by-hand way out (Copy the comment, Open post) stays. 3) "Send anyway" and "Schedule anyway" are removed (supersedes part 2 of DEC-049): no channel_restricted code, no `anyway` argument on findings.send/findings.schedule, no `acknowledge` on ReviewFlow.approve, no acknowledged-restricted skip in the gate. The column approvals.acknowledged_json (migration 318) stays, unread and unwritten. Owner-scheduled Feed replies still send unattended (parts 1, 3 and 4 of DEC-049 stand). 4) Migration 320 rewrites what is stored: the 'rules' capability entry is dropped, a publish_post/publish_reply restriction that only carried the AI/automation text goes back to needs_check with the detail "Not checked yet.", and a detail that carries the promotion note says "Not checked yet." and keeps its status.

## Reason

The owner decides what to post; the application is eyes and a helper. A restriction guessed from the wording of a community's rules cannot be told from a mistake, and it stopped the owner's own text with a sentence the owner did not ask for. Removing the block is simpler and safer than decorating it with a way through (lesson 308): with no rules reader there is no warning to mis-word, no heuristic to maintain, no capability that claims a check the app cannot do, and no button for a situation that cannot arise. Only what the site itself enforces can stop a press, because there the press would fail anyway.

## Consequences

A comment under a post of a community that forbids AI-generated content is sent when the owner presses Send; the community's own moderators remain the judge, as they always were. The Channels screen no longer has a Rules row, and a restricted capability now means only "the site does not allow this account" (labelled "Restricted"). The first assessment of a Reddit channel after the upgrade makes one request fewer. Stored capabilities were rewritten by migration 320 (verified on a copy of the owner's database: 50 capability entries in 5 channels became 45, 5 'rules' entries gone, 4 AI-ban restrictions back to needs_check, 1 promotion note cleared); history in the log and the AI's earlier draft rationales is not rewritten. approvals.acknowledged_json remains in the schema as a dead column.
