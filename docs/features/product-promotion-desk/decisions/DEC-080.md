## Context

Phase G of the publishing plan (docs/design/publishing-api-and-schedule-plan.md), which the owner's goal of 2026-11-27 took with its recommended defaults. It waited because Meta's pages do not say where one owner gets a Threads token that lasts without a server. They do say how a token of an hour becomes one of 60 days (one request with the secret of the Threads app) and how a 60-day token is renewed (one request, no secret). Threads takes a picture or a video only from a public address on the internet (decision D5 of the plan, a storage bucket of the owner's, is not made). Built on Meta's documentation read on 2026-11-27, before any request to the real API, as phases E and F were.

## Alternatives

1. Wait for the owner's Meta app before writing anything: the phase would stay unbuilt though its requests are documented.
2. The sign-in window of Threads with a redirect address: Meta's sample says Threads takes no localhost address, so it needs a public HTTPS address of the owner's and the secret kept for every sign-in.
3. Ask the owner to exchange the token by hand with a command: error-prone, and the secret would pass through a terminal.
4. Keep the app secret in the vault to exchange again later: not needed, a 60-day token is renewed without it.
5. Send pictures through a public storage bucket: waits for the owner's decision D5.

## Decision

1. A Threads account can be made with "How the app works with it" = "Through a developer app (Threads' own API)". It is an account of its own, with a key and no browser and no Instagram account to sign in through; the browser account of Threads stays as it is. Connector `threads-api` (src/connectors/threads/), fake server `fake-threads.ts`.
2. The key is a Threads access token. When it is saved the application asks Threads how long it lasts (`debug_token`). A token that lasts is kept as it is. A token of an hour is exchanged for a 60-day token with the "Threads app secret", a second field of the same form: the secret is sent to Threads once and kept nowhere, and a short token without it is refused with the reason. A token Threads says nothing about is kept as it is.
3. Keys that end are renewed by the application: a connector may have `keyToKeep` and `renewKey`; the scheduler job `api-keys` runs every six hours, also while automation is paused, and renews a Threads token once less than 45 of its 60 days are left (Threads renews only a token at least a day old). The new token replaces the old one in the vault. A token Threads no longer accepts is left; the account's sign-in check says so. No token and no secret is ever logged or returned.
4. A post is words with the link last in them (as on the browser way, DEC-036) and the approved topic as `topic_tag` (DEC-079). It is made in Threads' two requests: a container, then the request that publishes it once Threads says the container is FINISHED (asked up to 12 times, 5 seconds apart). Nothing is published when the container cannot be prepared. Only the publishing request can end as "unknown"; a lost answer is found among the profile's own posts or replies.
5. A picture or a video is refused before the send, in the editor and by the connector: Threads takes them only from a public address.
6. Replies under the profile's posts are read with `conversation` (all levels), a reply goes out with `reply_to_id`, a repost on the profile is not read as its post, a private profile's reply has no name. Likes, replies and views of a post come from its insights; followers from the profile's insights.
7. The channel's address must be the profile the token belongs to.

## Reason

Everything a text post, its replies and its numbers need is in Meta's pages, so the phase need not wait for the owner's app to be written, only to be proven. The token exchange inside the application is the one documented way to a lasting token that needs no server and no command line: the owner pastes two values once. Renewal without a secret means the application never has to keep the app's secret.

## Consequences

Unproven until the owner has a Meta app with the Threads use case: every request (nothing was tried against the real API), the names of screens in the eight steps, where the Graph API Explorer gives a Threads token, the shape of a refusal of the API (read in both the Graph's shape and the token calls' shape), whether a text container is FINISHED at once, whether a renewed token is another word, whether posts made before "Go live" are public. Not built: pictures and video, hiding a reply, the quota read (250 posts and 1,000 replies in 24 hours), a post kept by Threads for a time (Threads has no schedule in its API), a date on the account's page saying until when the token lasts (it is in the Log). Changed on the way: an API account never needs an account to sign in through; `ConnectorContext.pause`; `ApiAccess.onceSecret` and `keyName`; `apiPictures: 'none'` in the post form.
