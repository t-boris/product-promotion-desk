## Context

Item 3 of docs/design/ideal-promotion-app.md (the owner's goal of 2026-11-27). A channel was added and worked with no stated expectation, so nothing ever said whether it was worth the owner's weeks.

## Alternatives

1. A hard limit on channels in work: the owner has not answered how many (question 3 of the document)
2. A verdict proposed or given by the AI: the threshold already says it; the decision stays the owner's
3. Visits or sales read from the site's analytics as the trial's number: no analytics connection exists (DEC-002)

## Decision

A channel may have one running trial: what is expected (a sentence), a length of 2, 3, 4 or 6 weeks, planned hours a week, and the number that means "keep": comments, likes or points, or views under the owner's posts there (where the application reads them, DEC-056), the owner's posts that stayed up, or a number of the owner's own (sales, sign-ups) typed at the end. The AI may fill the form on a press from the product and the channel's own numbers; it never starts a trial and never gives a verdict. The Trial card stands above the channel's Results; a trial whose time is up raises one Inbox item until answered. Verdicts: keep; stop, which parks the channel (paused, nothing deleted, Resume brings it back); extend by two weeks. Earlier trials stay listed with what came out. The card says how many of the product's channels are in work; no hard limit is enforced.

## Reason

The research: one owner keeps up one or two channels, chosen after short trials with a threshold written before the result is known.

## Consequences

On channels worked in the browser a trial can only be judged by posts that stayed up or by the owner's own number. Hours are planned, not tracked. The weekly review does not yet mention trials.
