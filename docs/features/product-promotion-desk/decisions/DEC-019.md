## Context



## Alternatives

1. Let the AI invent every personal detail during the mission (rejected: it was the finding)
2. Ask the owner one question per field during the mission (rejected: many pop-ups, no overall strategy)
3. Owner types the whole profile by hand (rejected: the AI should do the thinking)

## Decision

An account holds a whole profile (first and last name, display name, user name, birth date, gender, place, about-text, website, picture), stored with a purpose (the owner's words) and a strategy (the AI's reading of the platform's audience and why this person fits it), with a one-sentence reason for every field. The AI proposes the whole profile at once; the owner edits and approves it once; an edit of an approved profile makes it a proposal again. A registration or profile mission starts only with an approved profile and types exactly its values: the AI never invents a personal detail, and asks the owner (ask_owner) for anything the profile lacks or the site refuses. The picture is made by an engine with an image tool (Codex, in a folder it may write to) from the description in the profile, or replaced by the owner's own file; the AI uploads it into a file input by name (browser_upload), never by path. Honesty rules of DEC-010 apply to the proposal: no unsupported credentials, no neutral-bystander voice, no real or famous names or likenesses, and a person that clearly differs from the other profiles on the platform.

## Reason

After the live dry runs both engines silently made up the name, surname, birth date and gender. The owner said that is a strategic choice that depends on what the profile is for, so the AI must propose it with reasons and the owner must decide; one approval per account keeps the work light.

## Consequences

New table account_profiles (migration 206), profile.* API, a Profile block in the account card, a new mission kind 'profile', a browser_upload tool, an AiTask.makeFiles mode for Codex. Picture generation costs the owner's Codex quota (about 13k tokens measured once). Real-site acceptance of the submit step is still unverified.</consequences>
