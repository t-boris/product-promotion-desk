## Context

To choose a channel (a subreddit, a group) the owner sometimes has to look at the platform, signed in as the account, and poke around. Until now only the Accounts screen could open the account's browser, and only on the login page. Thread links in Opportunities used the system browser, with no account session. The Channel address field also gave no hint for Reddit (a generic placeholder), although the platform was known.

## Alternatives

1. A search or discovery inside the app (the AI browses the platform and proposes channels): a bigger feature, not asked for now; the owner wanted to look around themselves.
2. Open the system browser: no account session, so the owner is not signed in as the account.
3. Read the whole page the owner is on and let the AI parse the channel from it: reads and stores page content the owner did not ask to share; one explicit address is enough.
4. One-off buttons per screen with their own URL building: duplicated rules; one shared control and one core method instead.

## Decision

One shared Open browser control, shown wherever a channel is chosen or shown (Connect a channel, Accounts of this product, suggested channels, the channel card, Opportunities and Publications, and a thread link as an extra). It opens the account's own browser (Internal or Chrome, as the account says), with its session, through accounts.browseChannels: on the typed address when the platform's connector reads it as a channel of that platform, else on the platform's page where channels are found (PlatformInfo.browseUrl: a preset field for named platforms, Connector.channelGuide() for prepared ones such as Reddit); with neither it says to type the address of the site first (no_browse_url). While the owner holds the browser the screen shows the same state as the sign-in one (the app waits, Show the window, Done). "Use the page I'm on" (accounts.currentPage) reads one thing from the owner's window: the address, cut to scheme, host and path (no query, no fragment, no credentials, http(s) only) and the title, with no script, text or picture, only while the owner holds the window, nothing stored, only the host written to the log; it fills the Channel address field, where the owner can edit it and Connect still validates. Reddit gets an example (r/yourcommunity) and a hint that says the community is meant, not the site.

## Reason

The owner chooses channels by looking at the platform, so the browser has to open where the choice is made, on the page for that, signed in as the account. The existing hand-off already gives everything else (control taken, the app waits, one sign-in check when the window closes), so the feature adds only the page to open and one explicit, minimal read. Rules stay true: the app never types in the owner's window, the AI gets nothing from this browsing, and an address typed for a platform must be on that platform.

## Consequences

New API: accounts.browseChannels({accountId, address?}), accounts.currentPage({accountId}); PlatformInfo.browseUrl; PlatformPreset.browseUrl for the nine named platforms; Connector.channelGuide?() and BrowserService.ownerPage?() as optional ports (Electron: the window's own URL and title; Chrome: the tab's record from the browser, no script; routing delegates). While the owner browses, monitoring and publishing of that account yield, as with the sign-in browser. The browse pages of the real platforms are checked by unit tests only; they can change on the sites and are one line each in presets.ts and the Reddit connector.
