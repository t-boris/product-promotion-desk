## Context

Phase A of publishing-api-and-schedule-plan.md promises "a group may be scheduled as a whole or spread, one version per slot of its channel". Previously only individual version cards had "Send at" / "Next free time", while the group card offered only "Adapt with AI", "Approve all", and "Send all now".

## Alternatives

1. Keep scheduling only on each individual version card: leaves phase A's group scheduling unbuilt and forces manual scheduling on every version of a multi-channel post.
2. Allow scheduling unapproved versions at the group level: diverges from the plan's rule that group scheduling sets times on approved versions matching what the owner saw.

## Decision

The group card gains two secondary actions for approved versions: setting one date/time on all approved versions (`postGroups.schedule`, "Schedule all (N)") and placing each approved version on the next free posting time of its channel (`postGroups.enqueue`, "Next free time each (N)"). Both enforce the same rules as single-version scheduling and group approval: only an approved version whose latest draft matches `seen` (and whose approval still matches the current content hash, account, and destination) receives a time; any version that cannot be scheduled (unapproved, changed after seen, already sent, stale approval, or channel without posting times / no free slot within a year) is named with its reason and left untouched while the rest receive their time. Channels without posting times are named on the card with direct links to add posting times. "Send all" stays unchanged (press-time snapshot, skipping future-scheduled versions).

## Reason

Completes the last unbuilt item of phase A on the existing group card without introducing a new screen, keeping approval binding and per-channel slot isolation intact.

## Consequences

Adds two group routes (`postGroups.schedule`, `postGroups.enqueue`), an optional `scheduledAt` field on `PostGroupResult` for local time formatting, and a compact schedule section on the existing group card. "Send all" keeps its press-time snapshot rule and continues to skip future-scheduled versions.
