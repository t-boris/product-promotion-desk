## Context

Phase I of the publishing plan. X's API (pages read 2026-11-28) is paid per use: a post about $0.015, a post with a link $0.20, a read of a post about $0.005 a day; prices may change. OAuth 1.0a is being retired, so the way that lasts is OAuth 2.0 with a code and PKCE: the owner allows the app in X's window, the access token lasts two hours, the refresh token is used once and gives the next pair. X lets an app reply only to a person who mentioned the account, and every read of replies or mentions is paid.

## Alternatives

1. OAuth 1.0a keys pasted by the owner: simpler, but X retires it on 2027-01-31
2. Catching the answer by watching the address of the page in the account's browser: differs between the built-in browser and Chrome, fragile
3. Reading mentions and replies on a schedule: every read is paid, and a reply is allowed only to a person who mentioned the account
4. Video through the chunked upload now: left until a real app proves the simple path

## Decision

An account of X with the kind "developer app" is connected by pressing “Sign in with X” after typing the app's Client ID: the application opens X's page in the account's own browser, waits ten minutes at most on http://127.0.0.1:47821/signin for X to send the browser back, and keeps what X gives only in the vault. The mechanism is general (a connector says it is connected by a sign-in and gives the page's address and the exchange); nothing of the exchange is logged. A key that ends within hours is renewed when it is used, one account at a time, and the daily renewal keeps an unused sign-in alive. The channel is the profile that signed in; it is for the owner's own posts, is not read and is never watched in a browser. A post is words with up to four pictures (JPEG, PNG or WEBP, 5 MB each) or one GIF; likes, replies and views are read under each post. The preview says what X charges for this send (with or without a link) and the day of the price list. Not built: reading replies and mentions and answering them, a video, a quote.

## Reason

The sign-in in X's window is the only way X keeps after OAuth 1.0a ends, and an answer to an address on this Mac works the same in every kind of account browser, while watching the page's address would not. The owner pays X for every request, so the application reads nothing on a schedule but the numbers of its own posts and says the price before each approval. Reading replies is paid and answering is allowed only when the account was mentioned: that needs the owner's word and a proof with a real app first.

## Consequences

The owner registers the callback address http://127.0.0.1:47821/signin in the X app (type Native App) and buys credits in X's Developer Console. Prices in the application are X's list of 28 November 2026 and are named with that day. Everything is built on X's documentation and a fake X: unproven with a real paid app. Comments on X are read and answered on X itself.
