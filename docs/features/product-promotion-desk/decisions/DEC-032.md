## Context

Until 0.17.0 a post belonged to exactly one channel: one opportunity, one draft chain, one approval chain, one send action. The owner (2026-11-15) asked to write a post once and put it on several channels (Reddit, Instagram, Facebook, Threads) with the AI suggesting and doing the cross-posting. The approval hash binds exact content, account, identity and destination, the gate and the sending flow serialize per account, and every guard (one open send per opportunity, one active approval per draft) is per opportunity. Pictures and non-connector platforms were made possible by DEC-030 and DEC-031.

## Alternatives

1. One approval record for the whole group: a single hash over all versions would break the per-account/destination binding and let one edited or failed version invalidate or smuggle the others
2. Group as the unit of sending (one send action for N channels): needs a new partial-failure model and touches the gate, the executor choice and reconciliation
3. Cross-post by copying the text by hand to each channel: no suggestion of channels, no per-platform fit, no single approval press
4. The AI picks and ticks the channels itself: the owner's rule is that the AI proposes and the owner decides where text goes

## Decision

(1) A post group is a wrapper only: a post_groups row holding the master text, and opportunities.group_id on ordinary standalone opportunities, one live version per channel (unique index, a deleted version may be made again). Each version keeps its own drafts, its own approval (its own exact hash), its own send and its own failure; the gate, the hash, the sending flow and the scheduler do not know groups exist. (2) The owner writes the master once and ticks channels; the app says per channel whether the post fits as it is and why not (rule-based, using the same render and fit functions as the approval), marks what it suggests and ticks nothing. "Also post to" turns an existing post into the first version of a group. (3) "Adapt with AI" asks the AI helper, per version, to rewrite the master in the limits of that platform; the result is stored as a draft by the AI (any approval of that version no longer applies), passes the checks every AI draft passes, and a version the owner changed by hand is left alone unless the owner asks to start over. (4) One approval press for the group: each version is approved by the same call as a single post, only for the draft the owner was shown (a map of version to draft id sent by the screen); a version that cannot be approved or changed since is named with its reason and left out, the rest are approved. (5) One send press: the approved versions go out one after another with a short pause, each through the ordinary send (gate, limits, executor); a version that fails or is held stops nothing else; the AI browser still carries a send only on the owner's own press. Nothing in the group code is reachable from the scheduler. (6) Deleting a group deletes its unsent versions; a sent version stays as an ordinary post.

## Reason

Keeping the unit of approval and sending the single post means every safety rule already proven (exact-content approval, per-account serialization, one open send, limits, the AI-browser boundary) holds unchanged for each channel, a partial failure (Reddit confirmed, Instagram needs the owner) never blocks the others, and nothing that was approved can be sent differently because it belongs to a group. One press for the group is a convenience on top of per-version approvals, not a new kind of approval.

## Consequences

The Publications screen gets a Where-to-post list and a group section; the single-post list hides versions of a group; every version of a group is also an ordinary post for the API (edit, schedule for connector channels, approve, send, delete). The channel suggestion is a rule, not AI. The pause between sends is a fixed 5 seconds. Per-account limits are shared by the versions on one login and are checked at each send, so a group of two channels on one login can be held by the limit for the second. Unproven until the owner tries: the AI adaptation quality on real products, and groups that include Instagram/Facebook/Threads through the AI browser on real sites (see DEC-031).
