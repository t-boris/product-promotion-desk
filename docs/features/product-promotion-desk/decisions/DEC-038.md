## Context

Owner answer D3 of the UI redesign (DEC-037, phase D): remove the one-controller lock on the account browser. Until now (DEC-025, DEC-026, DEC-028) ControlledSession gave the browser to exactly one party: opening the owner's window took control (UserControl), the internal browser hid the AI's watched window meanwhile, Chrome showed the AI's own page as the owner's window, and while the owner held it monitoring (monitor.ts), the send gate, sending, AI-browser missions, metrics, sign-in with the saved password, assessment, profile drafting and agent runs all waited or refused (TakeoverError, 'browser_busy', BROWSER_BUSY), and switching the account's browser was refused. The owner must close the window before the AI can work, which is the opposite of how a person uses two windows of one site. Sharing one session has real risks: cookies are shared, the owner may log out or switch account while a mission runs, and a site may sign out other tabs.

## Alternatives

1. Keep the lock (rejected by the owner, D3).
2. Re-read the sign-in by navigating the AI's own page before every press: it destroys the form the mission has filled (the pictures of an Instagram dialog, the typed fields), so the mission could never press.
3. A third, hidden probe page of the AI that re-reads the sign-in beside the mission page: needs a second page per browser (a visible tab in Chrome) and is not needed to be safe, because a refused press with a re-read afterwards ends the same way; kept as an option if undetected session changes prove common.
4. Watch cookie changes of the session as the signal: every site rotates cookies on every page, so the signal is noise; an address that is a sign-in, sign-out or account-switch page is the signal that means something.
5. Refuse any press after the owner navigated anywhere: opening a window loads a page, so a send in progress would be interrupted by the owner opening a window, which D3 forbids.

## Decision

1. The owner may open any number of windows on an account's session; the AI works in its own page of the same session; neither takes the browser from the other. Internal browser: every press of Open browser opens one more window on the account's partition (a window that already shows exactly that address is brought to the front instead); the AI keeps its own 1280x900 view in a hidden window exactly as it was, shown only when the owner asks to watch it, and an owner window neither hides nor shows it; nothing takes control. Chrome: the AI drives a page it created itself and never a page it did not create. Chrome's first blank page goes to whichever of the AI and the owner needs a page first; the AI's page is otherwise a window of its own opened in the background (behind the owner's), and the owner's window is a new window of the same Chrome profile. A page the AI's page opens (a sign-in pop-up) is the AI's; a tab the owner opens by hand, and a pop-up of an owner page, is the owner's. 2. The explicit Take control / Give control back (accounts.takeControl, accounts.releaseControl, TakeoverError, the gate's hold) stays for one case only: the owner wants the AI's own page to finish something (a CAPTCHA, a question from the agent). Its semantics are unchanged, and taking control also brings the AI's page to the front so there is something to finish; giving control back no longer closes any window. 3. What replaces the lock is the sign-in identity. BrowserSession.identityEpoch() counts what the owner did in the session that can change who is signed in: a main-frame navigation (every hop of a redirect included), the address a window is asked to open, a page adopted while already on such an address, or a background request (fetch, XHR, beacon; never an image or a script) of an owner window to an address that is a sign-in, sign-out or account-switch page (a pure classifier, isSessionAddress). What the AI's own page does is never counted. (a) ControlledSession refuses every write that needs a ticket (fill, click, uploadFile, publishStep) with SessionChangedError when the number moved since the AI's turn began, before anything reaches the page; reads, looking and scrolling go on. (b) A post mission notes the number before its sign-in check; after it moved the mission does no more writes, the authority gives no lease and no commit; when the mission ends without a press the app re-reads the sign-in: signed out gives a failed send with auth 'login' (the account is suspended as for any failed send, the approval is kept), a CAPTCHA gives auth 'captcha', another identity gives a failed send that names both, the same identity gives not_started (the approval is kept, press Send again), a check that fails gives not_started. A press that was already made is never taken back: the read-back of the site decides, and an unknown result blocks a resend. (c) A join mission presses nothing after it moved. 4. The waits that existed only because the owner's window held the browser are gone, because the window no longer holds it: the monitor, the gate, sending, missions, metrics, sign-in with the saved password, assessment, profile drafting and agent runs are not stopped by an open window. They still stop, with the same words and the same safety, after an explicit Take control. 5. Use the page I'm on: Internal reads the owner window used last (focus is tracked), no script. Chrome: the owner's pages are the pages that are not the AI's; one page is read from the browser's own record (address and title); with several, one fixed read-only expression (OWNER_TAB_PROBE) asks each whether it is showing and has the keyboard focus (the only script that ever runs in an owner's tab, and only then; it deviates from DEC-028's "no script"); the answer is used when exactly one page is showing, or exactly one has focus; otherwise the existing several_tabs refusal stays, because the page is ambiguous. 6. When the owner's last window closes the sign-in is looked at once (as before), but not while the AI works on the account. 7. Switching the account's browser is still refused while an owner window is open, now because the switch would close it, and still while the AI works or control was taken. 8. UI: OpenAccountBrowser is a plain button that opens a window, with Use the page I'm on beside it while a window is open; the holding panel, its warning, Show the window and Done are removed from it, and so is the claim that only one control of an account shows the block (open-browser-claims.ts); the explicit take-control state stays in the Accounts screen.

## Reason

The owner asked for it, and the lock was a safety simplification, not a safety rule: the rules that matter (the approval bound to exact content, the ticket for each write, the identity before a submit, one mission per account, the profile guard) are not about who has a window open. What the lock hid was that a session is shared. Making the sign-in the thing that is checked, and refusing a press (not a mission) when the check is out of date, keeps every failure in the safe direction (nothing submitted, the owner is told, the approval is kept where it is still valid) without the AI waiting for a window. Reading the sign-in only after a refusal needs no second page and never disturbs the form of a running mission.

## Consequences

Supersedes in DEC-025: 'The owner's own window is the same Chrome window: taking control stops the AI, Done gives it back.' In DEC-026: switching is refused 'while the owner holds its browser' (now: while an owner window is open, or control was taken, or the AI works; the reason for the window is that the switch closes it). In DEC-028: 'While the owner holds the browser the screen shows the same state as the sign-in one (the app waits, Show the window, Done)', 'While the owner browses, monitoring and publishing of that account yield', and 'Use the page I'm on ... only while the owner holds the window ... no script' (see 5). Everything else in DEC-025, DEC-026 and DEC-028 stays. New: BrowserSession.identityEpoch, SessionChangedError, the classifier isSessionAddress, a refused press after an owner's sign-in or sign-out page. Not detected by design: a session change that leaves no sign-in-like address or request (a logout by a request to a neutral address; a site that signs the account out of other tabs by itself; a change of cookies alone); then the press reaches the site with the session it has now, the site's own session binding refuses it or the post lands on the account that is signed in, and the application's read-back of the site decides (an unreadable result stays unknown and blocks a resend). The Reddit and universal connectors also read the identity again in the turn before they type. The sign-in is not re-read mid-mission; it is re-read when the mission ends without a press. Known limits: with two or more owner windows of one account and the keyboard focus in the app, Chrome cannot tell which page the owner is on, so Use the page I'm on says so (several_tabs); an owner who closes the AI's own Chrome window loses that page's state and the AI opens a fresh page next use; the owner's windows are never closed by the app when control is given back. Proven with real browsers (src/browser/coexistence-probe.ts, 28 checks, run on the installed Google Chrome and on the Electron bundle, 127.0.0.1 only): the AI's page and an owner window are two pages of one session (the same cookie), the AI types and sends in its page behind the owner's window, a turn in progress is not interrupted by the owner opening a window and moving in it, a sign-out page or a background sign-out request of the owner's window moves the epoch while the AI's own page going to a sign-out address does not, a write of a turn that began before is refused with nothing sent, and an explicit take-over still stops the AI. The owner's sign-in checks and the AI's page are serialised per account as before, so a check after a closed window waits for the AI's current step, not for the whole mission.
