## Context

Owner's screenshot 2026-11-15 (an Instagram post by the AI browser): the AI reached the last caption step with the picture inserted, then reported needs_owner. The draft held a separate Link; Instagram's post form has only a caption, and the application never publishes part of the approved content, so the AI correctly refused to leave the link out. Approve did not refuse it beforehand, the preview did not say what becomes of the link, and the AI-suggested draft carried the product website because the drafting prompts did not know the platform has no link field. The owner asked why it could not work out how to fill the Instagram post.

## Alternatives

1. Refuse Approve for a draft with a link on a platform that has no link field (rejected: the link is often wanted, and a refusal sends the owner to edit by hand what the application can place itself).
2. Put the link in the caption on every platform without a link field, Instagram included (rejected by the owner: a link in an Instagram caption is not clickable).
3. Let the AI decide per post where the link goes (rejected: the tool layer, not the model, decides what is typed; the approved content must be exact).

## Decision

Each platform form states where a draft's link goes (PlatformForm.link). field: the post form has a link field (Reddit, any site not listed, Pinterest, TikTok, YouTube). in_text: no field, but a link in the text works (Facebook, Threads, X, LinkedIn, Telegram): the link becomes the last paragraph of the text, unless the text already holds that very address (the scheme and host are read without their case, the path as written, a closing slash is nothing, never a longer address or another port), and it counts against the text limit. left_out: no field and a caption link is not clickable (Instagram): the link is not sent. The one function that renders a post for a platform (renderForPlatform: preview, Approve, check, groups, gate, send, mission) applies the rule to Markdown and older drafts alike, and says in the preview notes what became of the link. The mission is therefore handed a post without a link part, so it never looks for a link field. An older draft is judged by its connector as before, except that a link added to the text which takes it over the limit is refused at Approve and at the send (a draft that was over the limit without the link is not judged, as before). The disclosure of the author's connection is read from the text plus the link as the post shows it (RenderedPost.shownLink), as before. The approval hash binds where the link goes (linkPlacementOf: in_text:1 or left_out:1, only for a draft with a link on a platform with no link field; contentHash takes the platform as a required argument), so a draft approved before the rule is approved again, and Reddit, every platform with a link field and every draft without a link keep their hashes; RENDER_VERSION stays 1. The AI that writes a post is told the platform's rule (context.channel.linkRule, only in the drafting of a post, a showcase and an own update), and the group adaptation leaves room for the link.

## Reason

The owner decided on 2026-11-15: for Instagram leave the link out, for the other platforms without a link field put it in the text. The rule sits in the one function every step already shares, so what the owner approved is exactly what is typed, nothing of the approved content is dropped silently, and the AI is never asked to fill a field that does not exist.

## Consequences

A draft with a link on Instagram is approved and sent with the caption only, and the preview says the link is left out. On Facebook, Threads, X, LinkedIn and Telegram the link stands at the end of the text and a post that was within the limit without it can now be over it (the refusal says the number). A draft with a link on those platforms that was approved before 0.20.1 returns to review and is approved again, once. A reply's link on such a platform is added to the text with no note on the reply card (the AI never writes one). Unproven: how real Instagram, Facebook and Threads composers behave; the mission was only proved on local pages in real Chrome and Electron.
