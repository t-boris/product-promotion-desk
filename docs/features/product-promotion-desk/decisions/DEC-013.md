## Context



## Alternatives

1. Catalog only: faster, but 'what to do in the channel' stays with the owner
2. Catalog, plan and a file-upload step in site profiles so the application can publish photos and video after approval: much more work, cannot be verified without live accounts, higher risk of platform blocks; can follow later
3. A hand-written connector per platform like Reddit: brittle, unverifiable without live accounts

## Decision

The platform list gains named platforms (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, X, LinkedIn, YouTube, Telegram, Threads, Pinterest) next to Reddit and "Other website". A named platform is data only (sign-in page, domains, handle form, what a post can hold, a note): accounts and channels on it are served by the universal connector and need a reviewed site profile like any other site. A short handle ("@brand") is accepted as the address, and an address outside the platform's domains is refused. For each connected channel the AI can write a plan on request: steps with formats, what the owner prepares, how often, and what to check first. The plan is advice stored with the channel; the application executes nothing from it and nothing is published without the owner's approval. Uploading photos and video from the application is not part of this decision.

## Reason

The owner asked for more channels than Reddit (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and others, in principle any), and for the AI to work out what to do on each. The universal connector and site profiles already exist for "any site"; named platforms make the common ones easy to pick, and the plan answers "what do I do here". Dedicated per-platform connectors would add much unverifiable code for platforms that restrict automation. The owner chose this scope over a catalog alone and over a catalog with media upload.

## Consequences

Support for a named platform is reported only as far as it was actually seen (REQ-002): every ability stays 'needs check' until a site profile exists and the ability is observed.
