## Context

0.38.1 fixed the owner's Send under a Reddit comment, and the next Send under a comment of Grand_Extension_6437 (r/beginnerastrology) failed with "The browser blocked the Reply control under Grand_Extension_6437's comment, so the reply form needs to be opened manually". The post mission's click guard had two layers. Layer 1 refused, by NAME, every control named like one that publishes (Reply, Comment, Add a comment, Post, Save, Send, Submit, Publish, Share, plus Russian verbs) and every control the page marks as a native submit (`isPublishingControl`). Layer 2 (`handsOff`) refuses every click, choice or file the AI makes while ANY field of the page holds text or cannot be read. On Reddit's current layout the control that opens the reply form under a comment is a button named "Reply", so layer 1 blocked the one control that merely opens the form, and the brief ("a button that publishes is never clicked by you, it is refused") gave the AI no other road. The run record kept one line, so what the AI had tried before the refusal could not be read afterwards. The owner rejected the old.reddit.com route proposed in the first plan and said "Разреши AI нажимать на кнопки" (let the AI click buttons).

## Alternatives

1. Open the reply form on the old layout of the site (old.reddit.com) where the Reply control is a link (rejected by the owner: another layout of the site is a second thing to keep working, and Reddit's current layout is the one the account sees)
2. Allow a fixed list of opener names (Reply, Add a comment, Create post) and keep refusing the rest of the publishing names (rejected: the names differ by site and language, a list of allowed names is the same name-matching problem that caused the failure, and the guard that matters does not depend on a name)
3. Let the AI click the publishing control with text in the form when the application holds a ticket (rejected: only browser_submit types, reads back, looks again and presses once under the sealed authority; a second way to press would give up that single point)
4. Judge emptiness from the snapshot's editable values (rejected: the snapshot has no value for contenteditable text boxes, which is what Reddit's composer is; the DOM read of `handsOff` sees them, walks open shadow roots and same-origin frames, and already fails closed)

## Decision

1) In a PUBLISH mission only, the AI may click or choose a control named like one that publishes (Reply, Comment, Add a comment, Post, Save, Send, Submit, Publish, Share; a button, an input button, or a control the page marks as a native submit) when no editable field of the page holds text. The name check (`isPublishingControl`, the PUBLISHING_CONTROL refusal) is removed: it is not kept as a branch that can never fire. 2) The emptiness is judged by the existing held-text read (`handsOff`: textareas, text inputs, contenteditable and role=textbox elements, hidden mirror inputs, open shadow roots and same-origin frames), made when the click is made, from the page as it is then, never from a look made earlier. Blank and zero-width text count as empty. Any field that holds text, or a page whose fields cannot be read (an unreadable page, a script that throws), refuses the click exactly as before, with the same sentences. 3) The AI types nothing: the only text that ever enters a form is typed by the application inside `browser_submit`. So with every field empty a click on a publishing-named control can only open a form (or submit an empty form, which a site rejects). 4) The FINAL press stays `browser_submit`: the application types the approved text, reads every field back, looks at the page once more and presses the one control under the sealed authority, once. The AI never presses the publishing control of a form that holds text, because every click and choice is refused while any field holds text. 5) Every other guard is untouched: PICTURE_IN_FORM (with a picture in the form only Next/Continue are clicked), PROFILE_UNTOUCHED, NOT_PUBLISHED_HERE (join, follow, vote, block, delete, report, allow), POST_SETTING_CONTROL (community, flair, tags, NSFW, spoiler, schedule, crosspost, posting as), the file chooser guard, the host allowlist, `destinationMatches`, and the join mission's one-click allowlist. The watch and research missions have no clicks of this kind. `looksLikeSubmit` stays: `browser_submit` validates its submit_ref with it. 6) The mission brief says the true rule: the Reply control under the comment (a link or a button) opens the form and the AI may click it while the form is empty; then it looks again with browser_snapshot, finds the empty reply field and the button that publishes, and calls browser_submit once with their labels; "a control that publishes is clicked by you only while every field is empty, to open a form; after that only browser_submit presses it". The same for a new post ("Create post"). 7) A compact record of the mission's tool calls is kept (tool, the address opened without its query, the label of the control pressed, the sentence of a refusal; at most 25 steps of at most 160 characters; never the name of a text box, a typed or approved text, a field's content or the words of a page: the human check and the warning of the site are cut before their words). It is written as ONE log event (source `send`, level warn, with the channel) when a publication ends failed, unknown or stopped by the owner, e.g. "The AI's steps: 1. opened ... | 2. looked at the page | 3. clicked \"Reply\" (refused: ...)". No table and no migration: the event is an ordinary log row.

## Reason

What makes a press dangerous is text in a form, not the name of the control: the AI types nothing, and the held-text check already refuses every click while any field holds text, so with every field empty the name of the control tells nothing the application needs. A guard on the name also blocks the one control that opens the reply form on Reddit's current layout, and a brief that forbids the click leaves the AI with no road (lesson 309). The guard is kept on the step that can publish (a press with text in the form, which only browser_submit makes under a ticket). The step record answers the other half of the failure: a refusal without the steps before it does not say what the AI tried.

## Consequences

The AI can open a reply form (or the new-post form) by clicking a button named Reply, Comment, Add a comment, Create post and the like, on the approved page, while every field is empty. Residual risks, known and accepted: (a) the held-text read is not atomic with the mouse event, so a page that fills a form between the read and the click is not caught for that one click (the guard already had this limit for every other control); (b) text in a closed shadow root or a cross-origin frame cannot be read and is not reported as held (a limit of the page read, shared with every other check of the held text); (c) a control named like one that publishes and pressed with all fields empty is submitted as an empty form or does what it does with nothing typed (a site rejects an empty post; a control that acts without text, such as Save on a comment or a repost menu, is no longer refused by its name; the destination fence, NOT_PUBLISHED_HERE and POST_SETTING_CONTROL still apply, and the picture guard still applies once a picture is in the form). Unproven on the real site: Reddit's shadow-DOM Reply button under a comment and the composer that opens after it, on the live page. A publication that does not go through now leaves its steps in the log (one event). A fake page that makes the Reply button the submit button (as the old tests did) is not a test of the real flow: the flow test opens the form with an opener button, then lets browser_submit publish.
